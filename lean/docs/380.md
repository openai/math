# Strongly polynomial-time linear programming

The following describes the scope of the Lean formalization related to the following accompanying paper(s):

- [Strongly polynomial linear programming](../../preprints/Strongly-polynomial-linear-programming-November-24-2026/paper.pdf)
- [Superpolynomial graph diameter of simple polytopes](../../preprints/Superpolynomial-graph-diameter-of-simple-polytopes-November-24-2026/paper.pdf)

## Scope

Rational linear programming is solvable in strongly polynomial time.

Fixed rational RAM programs solve feasibility and optimization for $Ax\le b$ with $m\ge1$ constraints and $d\ge1$ variables. Instruction counts are polynomial in $m+d$, and stored rationals have bit length polynomial in the input bit length.

Feasibility returns a feasible point or a Farkas certificate with at most $r+1$ nonzero entries, where $r$ is the rank of $A$. Optimization returns an infeasibility certificate, primal and dual optima of equal value, or a feasible point and a ray with unit objective gain per step.

A family of simple rational polytopes disproves the polynomial Hirsch conjecture. It has $n$ facets for all sufficiently large $n$, subpolynomial dimension, and edge diameter that eventually exceeds every fixed polynomial in $n$.

For each positive subpolynomial $k(n)$ there is such a family even if one step can cross any face of dimension at most $k(n)$. For a chosen linear objective and each fixed $0<\alpha<1$, vertices whose values differ by at least $n^{-\alpha}$ of the full range are superpolynomially far apart. This includes the minimizing and maximizing vertices.

For every $a,b>0$ there are $c,\delta>0$ such that for all sufficiently large $d,e$, some simple rational $d$-polytope with $d+e$ facets has two vertices at distance at least $ce\min(e^a,d^b)$ even when steps may cross faces of dimension up to $\lfloor\min(d,e)^\delta\rfloor$.
